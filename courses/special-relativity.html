Special Theory of Relativity

The course I teach to third-year physics undergraduates at B.K. Birla College, Kalyan: from the postulates of special relativity to electromagnetism as a relativistic effect, space-time diagrams and an introduction to cosmology.

3 units · 3 sets of lecture notes · 6 assignments · every file opens as a PDF in a new tab

Unit 1Special Relativity: Kinematics and Dynamics

Galilean transformations and Newtonian relativity; why electromagnetism does not fit Newtonian relativity; the Michelson–Morley experiment and the ether hypothesis; the postulates of special relativity. Kinematics: simultaneity, the Lorentz transformation, length contraction, time dilation, relativistic addition of velocities, aberration and the relativistic Doppler effect. Dynamics: relativistic momentum and force, mass–energy equivalence, relativistic energy, and how momentum and energy transform.

Unit 2Relativity and Electromagnetism

How special relativity and electromagnetism are connected; the interdependence of electric and magnetic fields; the Lorentz transformation of the fields; the field of a uniformly moving point charge; forces and fields near a current-carrying conductor; the force between moving charges; the invariance and covariance of Maxwell’s equations under Lorentz transformations, and applications of relativistic electrodynamics.

Unit 3Space-Time Geometry and Cosmology

Space-time diagrams; simultaneity, length contraction and time dilation on them; the time order and space separation of events; the twin paradox; the principle of equivalence and general relativity; gravitational redshift. Cosmology: the large-scale structure of the Universe, types of galaxies, radio sources, quasars, the Doppler shift and the expansion of the Universe, Hubble’s law and the radiation background. Astronomy in different bands: optical, radio and X-ray.
