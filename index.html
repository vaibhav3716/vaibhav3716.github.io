About

I'm a physicist from Mumbai working in stellar astrophysics, and I'm looking for a PhD position where I can keep doing it. My main project, as a Visiting Research Student at the Tata Institute of Fundamental Research, is a search for white dwarf–main sequence binaries using ultraviolet data from GALEX and optical data from Gaia. It began as my MSc thesis and has grown into a catalog of more than five thousand systems.

Alongside it, I study how the black hole X-ray binary 4U 1630–47 changes during its outbursts, using archival RXTE data at St. Xavier's College. I also teach undergraduate physics at B.K. Birla College, Kalyan.

During my master's I worked with data across the spectrum: NuSTAR X-ray spectra of a black hole, VLA radio lines from a dying star's envelope, e-MERLIN and EVN images of an active galaxy, and gravitational-wave signals from merging black holes. Most of that work came back to the same objects: binary stars and the remnants they leave behind.

Current research

Finding white dwarfs hidden on the main sequence

MSc thesis, continued as a Visiting Research Student at TIFR Mumbai, Nov 2024 to present. Supervised by Prof. Sourav Chatterjee (TIFR), with Dr Anindya Ganguly (IUCAA, Pune) and Dr Prasanta K. Nayak (Pontificia Universidad Católica de Chile).

A white dwarf is the exposed core of a star like the Sun: about the size of Earth, very hot, and faint. When one orbits a cooler main sequence star, the companion outshines it in visible light, and the pair looks like a single ordinary star. Spectroscopic surveys miss most of these systems, and optical colour cuts only catch the pairs where both stars are about equally bright.

In the ultraviolet the situation reverses. A white dwarf at 15,000 K gives off most of its light below 3,000 Å, where a cool companion is nearly dark. I cross-match GALEX ultraviolet photometry with Gaia DR3 and look for stars that sit on the main sequence in the optical but on the white dwarf sequence in the ultraviolet. Then I fit each star's spectral energy distribution (SED) with two model atmospheres at once, one for each component, to measure both stars.

What the catalog shows so far

  • White dwarf masses for 3,213 systems, from 0.2 to 1.3 M☉, found by interpolating cooling tracks.
  • 306 possible extremely low-mass white dwarfs (below about 0.3 M☉). I treat these as targets for spectroscopic follow-up, not confirmed detections.
  • A magnitude-limited sample. Almost every system lies beyond 100 pc and well over half beyond 500 pc, so it reaches a population that 100 pc surveys can't.
  • White dwarf temperatures agree with five independent catalogs below about 20,000 K, across three different measurement techniques.

Timing and spectra of the X-ray binary 4U 1630–47

St. Xavier's College, Mumbai, Jan 2025 to present. Supervised by Prof. Manojendu Choudhury.

4U 1630–47 is a black hole X-ray binary that flares into outburst every few years. Using the full RXTE archive, I follow how its timing and spectral properties change together through every outburst cycle. Spearman rank tests between spectral and timing parameters turn up several statistically significant correlations, tied to when quasi-periodic oscillations (QPOs) appear.

Projects across the spectrum

Short projects from my MSc at St. Xavier's College. Each one used a different part of the spectrum, or no light at all.

RadioInfraredOpticalUVX-ray
  1. X-rayNuSTAR, XSPEC

    Spin of the black hole in M33 X-8

    I fitted NuSTAR spectra of the ultraluminous X-ray source M33 X-8 with diskbb, simpl and kerrbb models, and estimated a spin of about 0.85 (χ² ≈ 1.09). The result bears on how accretion works around stellar-mass black holes.

    Project report
  2. RadioVLA, CASA

    SiS molecular lines in IRC+10216

    I reduced VLA observations of the carbon star IRC+10216 in CASA, including flagging and calibration, and extracted spectral lines of SiS from its circumstellar envelope.

    Project report
  3. Optical to IRBagpipes

    Galaxy properties from multi-band photometry

    I estimated stellar mass, age, star formation rate, metallicity and dust content from photometry, using stellar population synthesis and Bayesian inference to quantify uncertainties and compare models.

    Project report
  4. Gravitational wavesBilby, GWpy

    Distances to merging black holes

    I ran Bayesian parameter estimation on binary black hole signals with Bilby, measuring chirp mass, mass ratio and luminosity distance, and used Bayes factors to weigh signal against noise.

    Project report
  5. Radio and X-raye-MERLIN, EVN

    A waking black hole in NGC 660

    I studied the recent radio outburst in the polar-ring galaxy NGC 660, about 13 Mpc away, using e-MERLIN and EVN interferometry with X-ray spectroscopy. The radio spectrum and jet-like features point to the nucleus becoming a low-luminosity active galactic nucleus.

    Project report
